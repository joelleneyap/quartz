# 2Q25

Hello,

Hope you're well! It's me again, having unfortunately missed the window for sending a 2Q25 update, but deciding to just combine one with 3Q25. It's clear that I've barely registered the summer just flying by - it's been a whirlwind both professionally and personally!

I will be hitting a year into my job at Altman Solon in about 10 days, and the number of interns and new Analyst hires our firm has welcomed in recent weeks has made me feel that tenure all the more. I've spent the past few weeks working on a project for a **Japanese media company across both inorganic and organic growth**! It was such a rewarding experience, to propose new ways for a brand that I've grown up with to expand its presence. This job can be a slog, but suddenly the late nights didn't matter because I believed so hard in my own ideas and the potential of this client. My goal is to seek out more work like this, with the limited freedom I have within my firm's staffing model, and to keep building relationships with the partners who bring in these media projects.

Before that, I spent most of the summer working on a **public safety project for a large telco, scanning the global landscape for possible acquisitions**. I realized that "public safety" can mean many different things to many different people. A drone that allows firefighters greater situational awareness of a fire on the rooftop of a building -- that can be the same drone deployed in suburban neighborhoods to do "proactive" (almost predictive) policing. As part of the project, we conducted many expert interviews, and I got to hear from both buyers (e.g. police chiefs) and vendors in the space. There was no place in their worldviews, I think, for the kind of critical analysis and 'doubting' of the good of technological innovation that I did in college / Data 104. [Fusion Centers](https://www.dhs.gov/fusion-centers) , for example, came up several times with no mention of the harm that widespread institutional data sharing can do to our ability to be free from state surveillance.

It made me uncomfortable, realizing that there isn't much I can do to introduce more critical thinking given my tenure at the firm and the fact that doing so doesn't align with profit-making assumptions that underpin work in consulting. It made me wonder if there is any way to do what I want to do professionally and ethically - as long as I'm working in corporate and I'm exchanging my labor for money, maybe there isn't a way to do work that is more than morally neutral at best. My logic goes: 
1. In a corporation, the individual exchanges labor for money, and the corporation's highest goal is profit, which always takes precedence over any other priorities 
2. Most jobs - excluding working in education, non-profits, etc - function within this system, and thus are at best morally neutral, given that profit is the unstated ultimate goal
3. But there are other ways to do good! In fact aiming to do good outside of the corporate structure should be the goal, since it limits our priorities as individuals so heavily. My contributions can be measured in terms of personal relationships, communities I've built or am part of, connections I've made and helped my friends make, and every deed I do consciously outside of the financial priorities imposed upon me by the system. I don't think there's any meaning to be found in aiming for a kind of "doing good" that must be validated by a system that uses money as its main quantifier of impact
4. What should I do for a career then? Something morally neutral, but that I enjoy doing, that allows me to circulate my energy in a way that I can feed back into my endeavors outside the job

And that leads me to the true takeaway of 2/3Q25: **Of the two options for my future path I laid out before, I've settled on trying to make the jump into media and entertainment**, in content acquisition / content strategy / corporate strategy. While working for a tech ethics company would both be doing good for the world and be intellectually interesting, media will always light my brain on fire in a way that nothing else can. Being employed by a media company might be morally neutral, but committing myself to something I'm tremendously passionate about, that extends beyond me and creates connections with other people -- I think there's good to be found there.
