Last year I watched The Minecraft Movie in an AMC packed to the brim with 12-15 year olds. At [the chicken jockey scene](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EY4h38NaXwU), I watched some kid get escorted out of the theater by staff because he'd hurled his entire bucket of popcorn *and* his drink at the screen. I later relayed this story to friends with lots of jokes around "kids these days" and their cinema etiquette - but thinking now, it was a symptom: Going to the movies is back in! But its post-Covid final form is one I still need to get to know.

2025 saw a 25% increase in theater attendance amongst us in Gen Z. Key here is the reasons we cite for this: concessions, premium screens, deluxe seats. ([Cinema United, Strength of Theatrical Exhibition, 2025](https://cinemaunited.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Strength-of-Theatrical-Exhibition-December-2025-Update.pdf)) Going to the movies is therefore differentiated against other things that we could do to entertain ourselves with visual content: 1) watching a movie at home on a laptop or a TV; and 2) other short-form screentime like TikTok and YouTube ([Vox](https://www.vox.com/culture/474836/netflix-warner-bros-movie-theaters-streaming)). Moviegoing is an in-person social experience in a public place.

And both exhibitors and content owners/producers have seen this coming a mile away. [AMC's Go Plan](https://investor.amctheatres.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/372/amc-theatres-announces-amcs-go-plan-a-multi-year-plan-to-invest-up-to-1-5-billion-over-four-to-seven-years-greatly-improving-the-movie-going-experience-at-amc), for example, was announced in late 2024. It will invest $1 - 1.5B over 4-7 years into "premium experiences": seating upgrades, theater renovations, IMAX® with Laser, Dolby Cinema, etc. Among content owners/producers, we're seeing pushes like:
* Concert movies e.g. [Eras tour](https://variety.com/2023/film/news/how-taylor-swift-eras-tour-concert-movie-landed-amc-theaters-123…)
* Musical movies converted into interactive sing-alongs e.g. [K-Pop Demon Hunters](https://www.amctheatres.com/movies/kpop-demon-hunters-a-sing-along-event-81838) 
	* This one has an added layer of interestingness because Netflix was in the comfortable position of *knowing* already that it would do well in theatres, based on the huge numbers it did on streaming, such that making it more interactive wasn't that much of a stretch
* Pairing tentpole movies to create a kind of 'unmissable event' - none other than [[barbie 2023|Barbenheimer]]
* Live streaming sports events e.g. [NBCU streaming the 2026 Winter Olympics](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/nbc-olympics-live-milan-coverage-amc-theatres-1236476568/) in AMC theaters 
* (Bonus: Back on The Minecraft movie, they did such a good job of social / viral marketing with this movie, by putting all of its most meme-able, iconic lines ("I... am Steve") on TikTok to the point that younger audiences basically could recite the whole movie from memory by the time they hit theaters. In that case maybe the [chicken-jockey meme ](https://variety.com/2025/film/news/minecraft-movie-theaters-warn-crazy-behavior-1236362844/)should have been something real / condoned / prepared for. We got a similar thing with the Gentleminions in 2022)

Looking forward though, I wonder if leaning into the premium "moving going as interactive social experience" could be a good defensive strategy for exhibitors not only against the rise of streaming, but also to hedge against the increasing leverage that studios/media companies have or are building. E.g., ignoring Sarandos' recent comments for a minute here ([honoring the 45 day theatrical window](https://www.msn.com/en-us/movies/news/netflix-to-keep-warner-bros-movies-in-theaters-for-45-day-window-says-ted-sarandos-i-want-to-win-the-box-office/ar-AA1Umm1p)), Netflix owning WBD could mean: 1) [less movies](https://variety.com/2025/film/news/theater-owners-netflix-buying-warner-bros-fewer-movies-ted-saran…) go theatrical; 2) more negotiation leverage for the ones that do.

In other words I'm wondering **if an LBE-like (Location Based Entertainment) approach to moviegoing would be the move, allowing higher prices and more hype around 'experiences' vs simple viewing, which I could do at home.** What this might look like:
* Significantly less seats, but all of them have good views of the screen
	* Maybe even smaller theater sizes in general, to 
* More legroom, cleaner, more couch-like, better quality concessions
* What Netflix did with K-pop Demon Hunters: test something out on streaming, confirm that it's going to be popular, then construct exclusive, time-windowed experiences around a guaranteed success
* Themed special events, e.g. a James Bond double-bill, complete with a black tie dress code and an interactive murder mystery coordinated with clues on physical tickets
* Specifically on franchise movies like the current marketing around Avengers Doomsday - have each trailer feel even more exclusive. Give out blindbox Avengers merch like its a concert, have an actor from one of the theme parks randomly appear at a theater showing the trailers to do a quick segment (not even a meet and greet, just shout an iconic line in full costume and disappear)
